# Research Backbone

The specification combines policy-rule literature with the stricter exchange-
rate-predictability literature.

- Taylor (1993), *Discretion versus policy rules in practice*. Establishes the
  policy-rule intuition linking inflation/activity states to policy rates.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2231(93)90009-L
- Clarida, Gali, and Gertler (1998), *Monetary policy rules in practice: Some
  international evidence*. Supports estimating reaction functions across
  central banks instead of assigning subjective scores.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0014-2921(98)00016-6
- Engel and West (2005), *Exchange Rates and Fundamentals*. Explains why
  exchange rates may behave like forward-looking asset prices even when
  fundamentals matter economically.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/431079
- Molodtsova and Papell (2009), *Out-of-sample exchange rate predictability with
  Taylor rule fundamentals*. Direct precedent for testing relative policy-rule
  fundamentals out of sample rather than assuming they forecast FX.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2009.06.001
- Rossi (2013), *Exchange Rate Predictability*. A broad review motivating
  strict holdouts, benchmark comparisons, and resistance to ex-post tuning.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.51.4.1063

The video framework discussed before this project is treated as an intuitive
diagnostic baseline: compare inflation and labour regimes across countries. It
does not determine model weights. SPD v1 estimates weights only against future
policy outcomes inside past-only folds, then tests FX association separately.
